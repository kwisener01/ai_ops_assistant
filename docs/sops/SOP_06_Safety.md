## 3. Safety Culture and Principles

### 3.1 Core Values
1. **Zero Harm**: Our goal is zero injuries
2. **Everyone is Responsible**: Safety is everyone's job
3. **Speak Up**: If you see something unsafe, say something
4. **Stop Work Authority**: Anyone can stop work if unsafe
5. **Continuous Improvement**: Learn from incidents and near-misses

### 3.2 Safety Hierarchy of Controls

**Order of Preference (most to least effective):**
1. **Elimination**: Remove the hazard entirely
2. **Substitution**: Replace with less hazardous option
3. **Engineering Controls**: Guards, ventilation, barriers
4. **Administrative Controls**: Procedures, training, signage
5. **PPE**: Personal protective equipment (last resort)

## 4. Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment

### 4.1 Hazard Identification Methods

**Ongoing:**
- Employee observations and reports
- Near-miss reporting
- Incident investigations
- Supervisor inspections
- Safety committee walk-throughs

**Formal:**
- Annual comprehensive risk assessment
- Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) for each task
- Process Hazard Analysis (PHA) for chemical processes

### 4.2 Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)

**When Required:**
- New job or task
- Process or equipment change
- After an incident
- Every 2 years (review existing JHAs)

**Procedure:**

**Step 1: Select Job**
- Prioritize high-risk jobs
- Include employee input

**Step 2: Break Job into Steps**
- List sequential steps
- Keep to 10-15 steps (not too detailed)

**Step 3: Identify Hazards**
- For each step, ask "What could go wrong?"
- Consider all hazard categories:
  - Physical (struck by, caught in, fall, electrical)
  - Chemical (inhalation, skin contact, ingestion)
  - Ergonomic (repetitive motion, lifting, awkward posture)
  - Biological (rarely applicable in manufacturing)

**Step 4: Determine Controls**
- Apply hierarchy of controls
- Specify PPE required
- Note special procedures

**Step 5: Document**
- Complete JHA form
- Review with employees
- Supervisor approval
- Post at workstation
- Train employees

**Step 6: Review and Update**
- Revise if process changes
- Annual review
- Update based on incidents or near-misses

### 4.3 Risk Assessment Matrix

| Likelihood | Severity: Minor | Severity: Moderate | Severity: Serious | Severity: Fatal |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Rare | Low | Low | Medium | Medium |
| Unlikely | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| Possible | Medium | Medium | High | Very High |
| Likely | Medium | High | Very High | Very High |
| Almost Certain | High | Very High | Very High | Very High |

**Risk Response:**
- **Very High**: Stop activity, immediate action required
- **High**: Action required within 24 hours
- **Medium**: Action required within 1 week, interim controls
- **Low**: Monitor, address during continuous improvement

## 5. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### 5.1 Required PPE by Area

| Area | Safety Glasses | Steel Toes | Hearing Protection | Other |
|------|----------------|------------|-------------------|-------|
| Production Floor | Required | Required | Required in marked areas | Cut-resistant gloves as needed |
| Warehouse | Required | Required | Not required | High-vis vest for forklift areas |
| Maintenance Shop | Required | Required | As needed | Welding hood, arc flash PPE |
| Office | Not required | Not required | Not required | None |
| Shipping/Receiving | Required | Required | Not required | High-vis vest |

### 5.2 PPE Program

**Hazard Assessment:**
- Conducted for each job/area
- Documented on PPE Assessment form
- Reviewed annually or when changes occur

**PPE Provision:**
- Company provides all required PPE at no cost
- Employee selects from approved options
- Replacement provided as needed (normal wear)
- Prescription safety glasses program available

**Training:**
- When PPE is first required
- When new PPE is introduced
- Covers: when required, what type, how to wear/adjust, limitations, care and maintenance

**Inspection and Maintenance:**
- Employee inspects PPE before each use
- Damaged PPE removed from service and replaced
- Proper storage in clean, dry location

### 5.3 PPE Specific Requirements

**Eye and Face Protection:**
- ANSI Z87.1 certified safety glasses required in all production areas
- Side shields required
- Face shields required for grinding, chemical handling (in addition to glasses)
- Welding helmets for welding and cutting operations

**Foot Protection:**
- ASTM F2413 certified safety-toe footwear
- Electrical hazard (EH) rated if working on electrical equipment
- Metatarsal guards for heavy material handling

**Hand Protection:**
- Cut-resistant gloves (ANSI A2 minimum) for sharp parts
- Chemical-resistant gloves for solvents and cleaners
- Leather gloves for general material handling
- No gloves near rotating equipment (entanglement hazard)

**Hearing Protection:**
- Required in areas >85 dBA (marked with signage)
- Earplugs or earmuffs provided
- Annual audiometric testing for exposed employees

**Respiratory Protection:**
- Only required for specific tasks (spray painting, etc.)
- Medical clearance required
- Fit testing required
- Maintenance and care training

## 6. Incident Management

### 6.1 Incident Categories

**Definitions:**
- **First Aid**: Minor injury requiring only basic first aid
- **Medical Treatment**: Injury requiring professional medical care
- **Restricted Work**: Employee can work but with limitations
- **Lost Time Injury (LTI)**: Employee misses work beyond day of injury
- **Near-Miss**: Could have resulted in injury but did not
- **Property Damage**: Damage to equipment or facility

### 6.2 Incident Response

**Immediate Actions:**
1. **Ensure Safety**: Secure area, prevent further harm
2. **Provide Care**: Render first aid, call 911 if serious
3. **Notify Supervisor**: Immediately report incident
4. **Preserve Scene**: Do not disturb unless safety risk
5. **Identify Witnesses**: List all who saw incident

**First Aid and Medical Care:**
- First aid kits located throughout facility (marked on map)
- Trained first aid responders on each shift
- Eye wash stations and safety showers inspected weekly
- Designated medical provider for occupational injuries
- Employee transported to medical facility if needed (supervisor or designated driver)

### 6.3 Incident Reporting

**Employee Responsibilities:**
- Report ALL injuries, even minor (no blame culture)
- Report near-misses (recognition for reporting)
- Complete Incident Report Form within 24 hours
- Cooperate with investigation

**Supervisor Responsibilities:**
- Complete Initial Incident Report immediately
- Notify Safety Manager within 1 hour of incident
- Conduct preliminary investigation
- Log incident in AI Ops Assistant
- Implement interim corrective actions

**Safety Manager Responsibilities:**
- Review all incidents within 24 hours
- Classify severity
- Determine investigation level
- Notify OSHA if required (hospitalization, amputation, fatality)
- File workers' compensation claim
- Track and trend data

### 6.4 Incident Investigation

**Investigation Levels:**

| Severity | Investigation Type | Timeframe | Team |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|------|
| Near-Miss, First Aid | Supervisor Investigation | 48 hours | Supervisor + employee |
| Medical Treatment, Restricted Work | Formal Investigation | 72 hours | Safety Mgr + Supervisor + employee |
| Lost Time, Serious | Comprehensive Investigation | 1 week | Cross-functional team, consider external expert |

**Investigation Procedure:**

**Step 1: Gather Information**
- Interview injured employee (if able)
- Interview witnesses separately
- Take photos of scene, equipment, PPE
- Collect physical evidence
- Review procedures and training records
- Check equipment maintenance records

**Step 2: Reconstruct Sequence of Events**
- Timeline of what happened
- Identify what went wrong
- Note any deviations from standard procedure

**Step 3: Root Cause Analysis**
- Use 5-Whys methodology
- Create Ishikawa fishbone diagram in AI Ops Assistant
- Identify all contributing factors
- Categorize causes (unsafe act, unsafe condition, system failure)

**Step 4: Develop Corrective Actions**
- Address root causes, not just symptoms
- Apply hierarchy of controls
- Assign responsibility and due dates
- Prioritize based on effectiveness
- Consider horizontal deployment (similar operations)

**Step 5: Documentation**
- Complete Investigation Report
- Log in AI Ops Assistant for tracking
- Share learnings (toolbox talk, safety meeting)
- File report per retention requirements

**Step 6: Follow-Up**
- Monitor implementation of corrective actions
- Verify effectiveness (30 and 60 days)
- Update JHA if applicable
- Close investigation

## 7. Emergency Preparedness and Response

### 7.1 Emergency Types

**Potential Emergencies:**
- Fire
- Chemical spill
- Medical emergency
- Severe weather (tornado)
- Active threat
- Utility failure (power, gas, water)

### 7.2 Emergency Response Team

**Roles:**
- **Emergency Coordinator**: Safety Manager (primary), Plant Manager (backup)
- **Floor Wardens**: One per department (conduct evacuations)
- **First Aid Responders**: Minimum 2 per shift
- **Fire Brigade**: Trained in fire extinguisher use (entire team)
- **Spill Response Team**: Trained in chemical cleanup

### 7.3 Emergency Procedures

**Fire Emergency:**
1. Activate fire alarm (pull station)
2. Call 911 (do not assume someone else will)
3. Evacuate building (use nearest exit, do not use elevators)
4. Close doors behind you (do not lock)
5. Assemble at designated muster point (parking lot)
6. Floor wardens account for personnel
7. Do not re-enter until all clear given by fire department

**Fire Extinguisher Use (PASS):**
- **P**ull the pin
- **A**im at base of fire
- **S**queeze the handle
- **S**weep side to side
- Only fight small fires, evacuate if not extinguished in 30 seconds

**Chemical Spill:**
- Small spill (<1 gallon): Use spill kit, clean up, report to supervisor
- Large spill (>1 gallon): Evacuate area, call Spill Response Team, notify Safety Manager
- Refer to SDS for specific chemical hazards
- Use appropriate PPE
- Contain spill (absorbent, dikes)
- Dispose of waste per regulations
- Document incident

**Medical Emergency:**
- Call 911 for serious injuries or illness
- Begin first aid/CPR if trained
- Use AED if cardiac arrest (located near lunchroom)
- Notify supervisor
- Meet and direct EMS to patient
- Complete incident report

**Severe Weather (Tornado Warning):**
- Move to designated shelter areas (interior rooms, lower levels)
- Stay away from windows
- Sit on floor, cover head
- Remain until all clear

**Active Threat:**
- Run: Evacuate if safe to do so
- Hide: Lock doors, barricade, silence phones, turn off lights
- Fight: Last resort, use anything as weapon
- Call 911 when safe

### 7.4 Emergency Drills

**Frequency:**
- Fire evacuation: Quarterly
- Severe weather: Annually
- Active threat: Annually
- Spill response: Annually (team training)

**Drill Process:**
- Announce drill or conduct unannounced
- Observe response and timing
- Debrief with team
- Identify improvement opportunities
- Document drill and results
- Update procedures as needed

### 7.5 Emergency Equipment

**Fire Protection:**
- Fire extinguishers inspected monthly (tag signed)
- Fire alarm system tested quarterly
- Sprinkler system inspected annually
- Exit lights tested monthly
- Emergency lighting tested monthly

**First Aid:**
- First aid kits inspected monthly, restocked as needed
- AED inspected monthly, pads replaced per expiration
- Eye wash stations tested weekly (log maintained)
- Safety showers tested weekly

**Spill Response:**
- Spill kits located in chemical storage areas
- Contents: absorbent pads, socks, granules, gloves, bags, instructions
- Inspected quarterly

## 8. Safety Training

### 8.1 New Hire Safety Orientation

**Duration**: 4 hours (before any work begins)

**Topics:**
- Safety policy and expectations
- Emergency procedures and evacuation routes
- PPE requirements
- Hazard communication (chemicals, labeling, SDS)
- Lockout/Tagout overview
- Incident reporting
- Stop work authority
- Tour of facility (exits, equipment, hazards)

### 8.2 Job-Specific Training

**Before performing task:**
- Review Job Hazard Analysis (JHA)
- Demonstrate task with trainer
- Practice under supervision
- Supervisor sign-off on competency

### 8.3 Ongoing Training

**Annual (All Employees):**
- Safety refresher (2 hours)
- Emergency procedures
- Hazard communication update

**As Needed:**
- New equipment or process
- After incidents (lessons learned)
- When deficiencies identified

**Specialized Training:**
- Forklift operation: Initial 8 hours, annual evaluation
- Lockout/Tagout: Initial 4 hours, annual refresher
- Confined space entry: Initial 8 hours, annual refresher
- Fall protection: Initial 4 hours, annual refresher
- Powered industrial trucks: Per OSHA requirements
- First aid/CPR: Initial certification, renewal every 2 years

### 8.4 Training Documentation

**Records Include:**
- Employee name
- Training topic
- Date and duration
- Trainer name
- Method (classroom, hands-on, online)
- Competency assessment (pass/fail)

**Retention**: Duration of employment + 3 years

## 9. Safety Inspections and Audits

### 9.1 Inspection Types

**Daily:**
- Supervisor walk-through of area
- Observe PPE compliance
- Identify obvious hazards
- Correct immediately or document

**Weekly:**
- Safety committee inspection (rotating areas)
- Use checklist
- Document findings
- Assign corrective actions

**Monthly:**
- Comprehensive area inspection by Safety Manager
- In-depth review of high-risk areas
- Trend analysis of findings

**Annual:**
- Third-party safety audit
- Regulatory compliance audit
- Emergency equipment testing

### 9.2 Inspection Procedure

**Step 1: Preparation**
- Review area layout and processes
- Review previous inspection findings
- Gather checklist and tools (camera, meter, etc.)
- Schedule to observe operations

**Step 2: Inspection Execution**
- Walk through systematically
- Observe work practices
- Talk with employees (any concerns?)
- Check equipment, guards, PPE
- Review documentation (permits, training records, inspections)
- Photograph hazards

**Step 3: Documentation**
- Complete inspection report
- Rate findings by risk level (high, medium, low)
- Describe hazard clearly
- Suggest corrective action
- Assign to responsible party
- Set due date based on risk

**Step 4: Follow-Up**
- Track corrective actions in AI Ops Assistant
- Verify completion
- Close findings when verified
- Communicate results to area

### 9.3 Safety Audit

**Audit Scope:**
- Compliance with OSHA regulations
- Adherence to company safety policies
- Effectiveness of safety programs
- Training records and competency
- Incident investigation quality
- Emergency preparedness

**Audit Process:**
- Opening meeting with management
- Document review
- Facility walk-through
- Employee interviews
- Closing meeting (findings presented)
- Corrective action plan development
- Follow-up audit (verify closure)

## 13. Performance Metrics

### 13.1 Leading Indicators (Proactive)
- Near-miss reports submitted
- Safety training hours completed
- Inspection findings corrected on time
- JHAs completed and updated
- Safety meeting participation

### 13.2 Lagging Indicators (Reactive)
- Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR)
- Days Away, Restricted, or Transferred (DART) rate
- Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR)
- Severity rate
- Workers' compensation costs

### 13.3 Calculation Formulas

**Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR):**
```
TRIR = (Number of recordable injuries × 200,000) / Total hours worked
```

**Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR):**
```
LTIFR = (Number of lost time injuries × 200,000) / Total hours worked
```

**Targets:**
- TRIR: <2.0 (industry average ~3.5)
- LTIFR: <1.0
- Near-miss reporting: >50 per year (indicates good reporting culture)
