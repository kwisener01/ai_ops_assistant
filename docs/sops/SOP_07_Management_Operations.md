## 3. Strategic Planning and Goal Setting

### 3.1 Annual Strategic Planning Process

**Timeline**: Q4 of each year for following year

**Phase 1: Environmental Scan (October)**
- Review market trends and customer forecasts
- Analyze competitive landscape
- Assess internal capabilities and gaps
- Identify opportunities and threats
- SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats)

**Phase 2: Goal Setting (November)**
- Establish strategic objectives (3-5 year vision)
- Define annual goals aligned to strategy
- Set SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- Categories:
  - Financial (revenue, cost, margin)
  - Quality (defect rate, customer satisfaction)
  - Delivery (on-time, lead time)
  - Safety (incident rate)
  - People (engagement, turnover, training)
  - Innovation (new products, process improvements)

**Phase 3: Planning and Budgeting (December)**
- Develop initiatives to achieve goals
- Create project plans with milestones
- Build budget (capital and operating expenses)
- Allocate resources
- Identify risks and mitigation plans

**Phase 4: Communication and Alignment (January)**
- Cascade goals to departments and individuals
- Communicate plan to all employees
- Align performance management system
- Launch initiatives

**Phase 5: Execution and Monitoring (Throughout Year)**
- Track progress monthly
- Review metrics and dashboards
- Adjust plans as needed
- Celebrate wins and learn from setbacks

### 3.2 Dan Sullivan Strategic Thinking Frameworks

**The Gap and The Gain:**
- **Traditional (The Gap)**: Measure against ideal future state → demotivating
- **Recommended (The Gain)**: Measure against past performance → motivating
- **Application**: In reviews, focus on progress made, not just how far from goal

**10× vs 2× Thinking:**
- **2× Thinking**: Incremental improvements, doing more of the same
- **10× Thinking**: Breakthrough innovations, doing things fundamentally differently
- **Application**: Challenge teams to identify 10× opportunities, not just 2× improvements
- **Example**: Instead of reducing defects by 10% (2×), ask "How could we eliminate defects entirely?" (10×)

**Who Not How:**
- **Traditional**: "How do I accomplish this goal?" (time/energy intensive)
- **Recommended**: "Who can help me accomplish this goal?" (leverage expertise)
- **Application**: Build teams, delegate, partner with experts rather than doing everything yourself

**Free Zone Frontier:**
- **Competitive Zone**: Constrained by competitors and market norms
- **Free Zone**: Create unique value beyond competition
- **Application**: Innovate to create differentiation, don't just compete on price/features

## 4. Performance Management System

### 4.1 Individual Performance Management

**Annual Cycle:**

**Q1: Goal Setting (January)**
- Employee and manager collaborate on goals
- Align individual goals to department and plant objectives
- Document in performance management system
- Review and approve

**Q2 & Q3: Mid-Year Check-Ins (Quarterly)**
- Review progress on goals
- Provide coaching and feedback
- Adjust goals if business needs change
- Identify development opportunities

**Q4: Annual Review (November-December)**
- Employee self-assessment
- Manager assessment
- Performance rating (1-5 scale)
- Compensation decisions tied to performance
- Create development plan for next year

**Ongoing: Continuous Feedback**
- Regular one-on-one meetings (weekly for direct reports, monthly for skip level)
- Real-time recognition and coaching
- Documented in performance notes

### 4.2 Performance Rating Scale

| Rating | Description | Distribution Target |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| 5 - Exceptional | Far exceeds expectations, top performer | 5% |
| 4 - Exceeds | Consistently exceeds expectations | 20% |
| 3 - Meets | Fully meets all expectations | 60% |
| 2 - Needs Improvement | Partially meets expectations, development needed | 10% |
| 1 - Unsatisfactory | Does not meet expectations, performance plan required | 5% |

### 4.3 Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)

**When Required:**
- Rating of 2 or 1
- Specific performance deficiency
- Policy violation

**Procedure:**
1. Document performance gap clearly
2. Meet with employee to discuss
3. Create written PIP with:
   - Specific performance expectations
   - Support to be provided (training, coaching, resources)
   - Timeline (typically 60-90 days)
   - Consequences if not improved
4. Weekly check-ins during PIP period
5. Final assessment at end of period
6. Outcome: Improvement (continue employment) or Termination

## 5. Daily Management System (DMS)

### 5.1 Visual Management

**Purpose**: Make performance and issues visible to all

**Visual Tools:**
- **Performance Boards**: Display key metrics updated daily/weekly
- **Problem-Solving Boards**: Track active issues and corrective actions
- **Standard Work**: Posted at workstations
- **Safety Scoreboards**: Days without incident
- **Production Schedules**: Visible to all team members

**Location**: Posted at Gemba (where the work happens)

### 5.2 Daily Tier Meetings

**Three-Tier Meeting Structure:**

**Tier 1: Team Level (15 minutes at shift start)**
- **Participants**: Frontline team and supervisor
- **Agenda**:
  - Safety moment
  - Yesterday's performance (units, quality, safety)
  - Today's plan and targets
  - Open issues and needs
  - Recognize good performance
- **Output**: Issues escalated to Tier 2

**Tier 2: Department Level (15 minutes, mid-morning)**
- **Participants**: Supervisors and department manager
- **Agenda**:
  - Roll-up of Tier 1 results
  - Issues requiring cross-functional support
  - Resource needs
  - Progress on improvement projects
- **Output**: Issues escalated to Tier 3, assignments made

**Tier 3: Plant Level (30 minutes, late morning)**
- **Participants**: Department managers and plant manager
- **Agenda**:
  - Plant performance overview (safety, quality, delivery, cost)
  - Critical issues requiring senior leadership attention
  - Strategic initiative updates
  - Decision-making on resource allocation
- **Output**: Decisions communicated, actions assigned

**Meeting Discipline:**
- Start and end on time
- Stand-up meetings (keeps them short)
- Use visual boards
- Focus on facts and data, not opinions
- Document actions with owner and due date
- Follow up on previous actions

### 5.3 Gemba Walks

**Purpose**: Observe work at the source, engage employees, learn

**Frequency**:
- Supervisors: Daily in their area
- Department Managers: 3× per week
- Plant Manager: Weekly

**Approach:**
- **Go to Gemba**: Don't manage from office, see reality
- **Ask Questions**: "What are you working on? Any problems? What would make your job easier?"
- **Observe**: Watch for safety, quality, efficiency
- **Listen**: Understand employee perspective
- **Coach**: Provide feedback, teach problem-solving
- **Follow Up**: Act on issues raised

**Avoid:**
- Blaming or criticizing in front of peers
- Solving problems for them (coach them to solve)
- Rushing through
- Only visiting when there's a problem

## 7. Financial Management

### 7.1 Budgeting Process

**Annual Budget Development (Q4):**
- Review historical performance
- Incorporate strategic initiatives
- Build from bottom-up (department budgets)
- Validate assumptions (volume, pricing, inflation)
- Balance revenue and expenses to profit target
- Obtain approval from corporate/board
- Load into financial system

**Budget Categories:**
- Revenue (units × price)
- Direct labor (headcount × loaded rate)
- Materials (unit cost × volume)
- Overhead (utilities, maintenance, supplies)
- Capital expenditures (equipment, facility)

### 7.2 Monthly Financial Reviews

**Variance Analysis:**
- Compare actual to budget
- Explain variances >5% or >$10K
- Categorize: volume, price, efficiency, timing
- Identify corrective actions if unfavorable

**Key Financial Metrics:**
- Revenue vs. budget
- Gross margin percentage
- Operating expense ratio
- Earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT)
- Working capital (inventory + receivables - payables)
- Cash flow

**Actions:**
- Address unfavorable variances
- Reforecast if significant changes
- Communicate to senior leadership

### 7.3 Capital Expenditure (CapEx) Management

**CapEx Request Process:**
1. Identify need (capacity, cost reduction, safety, compliance)
2. Develop business case:
   - Project description and justification
   - Cost estimate (equipment, installation, startup)
   - Benefits (cost savings, revenue increase, risk mitigation)
   - ROI calculation (payback period, NPV, IRR)
   - Risks and alternatives
3. Submit for approval per authorization limits:
   - <$25K: Plant Manager
   - $25K-$100K: Operations Director
   - $100K-$500K: VP Operations
   - >$500K: Executive Committee or Board
4. Once approved, execute project
5. Track actual costs vs. budget
6. Conduct post-implementation review (benefits realized?)

## 8. Talent Management

### 8.1 Recruitment and Selection

**Process:**
1. **Requisition**: Manager submits hiring request with justification
2. **Job Description**: Define role, responsibilities, qualifications
3. **Sourcing**: Post internally and externally, recruit actively
4. **Screening**: HR reviews resumes, selects candidates for interview
5. **Interviewing**:
   - Phone screen (HR)
   - Behavioral interviews (hiring manager + team members)
   - Skills assessment (technical test, simulation)
   - Reference checks (previous supervisors)
6. **Offer**: Extend offer, negotiate if needed
7. **Onboarding**: Comprehensive orientation and training

**Interview Best Practices:**
- Use structured behavioral questions (STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result)
- Diverse interview panel
- Assess both skills and cultural fit
- Take notes, compare candidates objectively
- Check references thoroughly

### 8.2 Employee Development

**Development Methods:**
- **On-the-Job Training**: Mentoring, job shadowing, stretch assignments
- **Formal Training**: Classroom, online courses, certifications
- **Cross-Training**: Exposure to other functions, job rotation
- **Conferences and Seminars**: Industry events, professional development
- **Coaching**: Regular feedback, development discussions

**Individual Development Plans (IDP):**
- Annual conversation about career aspirations
- Identify skills to develop
- Create plan with specific actions and timeline
- Manager supports and provides opportunities
- Review progress quarterly

**Leadership Development:**
- Identify high-potential employees
- Accelerated development (special projects, leadership training)
- Succession planning for critical roles
- Mentoring from senior leaders

### 8.3 Employee Engagement

**Engagement Activities:**

## 9. Change Management

### 9.1 Change Management Process

**Types of Change:**
- Strategic (new markets, products, business model)
- Operational (process improvements, system implementations)
- Organizational (restructuring, leadership changes)
- Cultural (values, behaviors)

**Change Management Steps:**

**Step 1: Create Urgency**
- Communicate why change is needed
- Show risks of not changing
- Create burning platform

**Step 2: Build Coalition**
- Identify champions and influencers
- Get leadership alignment
- Engage skeptics

**Step 3: Develop Vision**
- Paint picture of future state
- Articulate benefits
- Address "what's in it for me?"

**Step 4: Communicate**
- Frequent, transparent communication
- Multiple channels
- Two-way dialogue, listen to concerns

**Step 5: Remove Barriers**
- Identify obstacles (processes, systems, people, skills)
- Provide training and resources
- Address resistance

**Step 6: Create Short-Term Wins**
- Quick successes build momentum
- Celebrate early victories
- Visible progress

**Step 7: Sustain Momentum**
- Don't declare victory too soon
- Continue driving change
- Adapt based on feedback

**Step 8: Anchor in Culture**
- Reinforce new behaviors
- Align systems (rewards, recognition, performance management)
- Make it "the way we do things"

### 9.2 Resistance Management

**Common Reasons for Resistance:**
- Lack of understanding (communicate more)
- Fear of unknown (provide information, reassurance)
- Loss of control (involve in planning)
- Bad timing (too much change at once)
- Past failures (rebuild trust)

**Strategies:**
- Involve people early
- Listen to concerns
- Address root causes
- Provide training and support
- Be patient but persistent

## 10. Risk Management

### 10.1 Enterprise Risk Assessment

**Risk Categories:**
- **Strategic**: Market changes, competitive threats
- **Operational**: Equipment failure, supply chain disruption, quality issues
- **Financial**: Currency, commodity prices, credit
- **Compliance**: Regulatory changes, audits
- **Reputational**: Product recalls, negative publicity
- **Safety**: Injuries, environmental incidents

**Risk Assessment Process:**
1. Identify risks (brainstorm, historical review)
2. Assess likelihood and impact (risk matrix)
3. Prioritize high-priority risks
4. Develop mitigation plans
5. Monitor and review quarterly

**Risk Mitigation Strategies:**
- **Avoid**: Eliminate activity causing risk
- **Reduce**: Implement controls to lower likelihood or impact
- **Transfer**: Insurance, outsourcing, contracts
- **Accept**: Acknowledge and monitor

### 10.2 Business Continuity Planning

**Purpose**: Ensure operations can continue after disruption

**Plan Components:**
- Critical business functions identified
- Recovery time objectives (RTO) defined
- Alternate suppliers and equipment identified
- Emergency response procedures
- Communication plan
- Data backup and recovery

**Testing:**
- Annual tabletop exercise
- Test critical systems (IT recovery, alternate suppliers)
- Update plan based on lessons learned

## 12. Continuous Improvement Culture

### 12.1 Lean Principles

**Core Concepts:**
- **Value**: Define from customer perspective
- **Value Stream**: Map end-to-end process
- **Flow**: Eliminate waste, create continuous flow
- **Pull**: Produce based on customer demand, not forecast
- **Perfection**: Continuous improvement, never satisfied

**Types of Waste (Muda):**
1. Overproduction
2. Waiting
3. Transport
4. Over-processing
5. Inventory
6. Motion
7. Defects
8. Underutilized talent

### 12.2 Continuous Improvement Program

**Idea Generation:**
- Encourage all employees to submit ideas
- Suggestion system (online or physical suggestion box)
- Kaizen events
- Problem-solving teams

**Idea Evaluation:**
- Review weekly
- Assess feasibility and impact
- Prioritize (impact vs. effort)
- Provide feedback to submitter

**Implementation:**
- Assign owner
- Pilot test if uncertain
- Document standard work
- Train employees
- Measure results

**Recognition:**
- Thank employee publicly
- Monetary awards for significant savings
- Recognition board highlighting implemented ideas
- Annual awards ceremony

### 12.3 Lessons Learned

**Process:**
- After projects, incidents, or significant events
- Ask: What went well? What didn't? What would we do differently?
- Document insights
- Share with organization
- Incorporate into future planning
